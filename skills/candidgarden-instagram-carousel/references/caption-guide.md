# Caption and music guide

Write the caption after reading `manifest.json` and inspecting the cards. The
caption is editorial work, not renderer output.

## Required shape

```text
[One- or two-line thematic hook]

Docent note — [Two to four sentences connecting the works. State what can be observed or what the catalogue records; do not invent iconography, biography, or collection history.]

[Sense disclosure when applicable.]

Works in order
01 — Title — Artist, period
Collection · candidgarden.com/archive/ID
...

Music — “Track” — Artist
[One short sentence explaining the tonal or conceptual fit.]

#candidgarden #artresearch ...
```

## Voice

Deadpan institutional, precise, welcoming, and quietly curious. Prefer short
sentences. Avoid promotional claims, stacked rhetorical questions, emojis,
exclamation marks, and phrases such as `AI-powered insights`.

The docent note may interpret relationships across the selection, but it must
distinguish visual observation from catalogue data and machine-written reading
proximity. Motifs in the manifest are provisional annotations, not verified
subject matter.

## Artwork list

Keep the posting order byte-for-byte aligned with the numeric PNG prefixes. Use
`Unattributed`, `Undated`, and `Collection not recorded` rather than silently
omitting gaps. Include each archive URL even though Instagram does not make
caption URLs clickable; the record IDs remain citable.

## Hashtags

Use 8-12 total. Always include `#candidgarden` and `#artresearch`. Add specific
terms supported by the actual selection, mixing broad discovery and narrow
scholarly tags. Do not add artist, museum, movement, or period tags absent from
the manifest.

## Music

Recommend one existing track whose pacing, instrumentation, title, or cultural
register fits the deck. Give title and artist plus one sentence of rationale.
Verify current availability only if the user asks for a platform link; otherwise
do not imply that a particular Instagram audio catalogue carries it.
